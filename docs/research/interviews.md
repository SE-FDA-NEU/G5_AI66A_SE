## Interview 1 — first-year student

- **Role:** first-year student, started this term.
- **Money:** about 4,000,000 ₫ a month in total.
- **Where it goes:** about 3,000,000 ₫ on food, because they rarely cook; about 1,000,000 ₫ on
  things that come up.
- **Records spending:** no.
- **Limit:** yes, but only in their head, never written down.
- **Ran out of money earlier than planned:** no.

## Interview 2 — studies and works

### Facts

- **Role:** studies and works at the same time.
- **Spends on:** rent, food, coffee and going out with friends, everyday necessities, and a sum set aside for costs that were not planned.
- **Ran out of money earlier than planned:** no. The participant said they usually know how much money they have and balance their overall monthly spending around it.
- **Records spending:** yes, through an e-wallet that lists spending by category each month. They rebalance from that list.
- **Limit:** money is split into small funds, one shared with friends and one personal, and they spend within each. Sometimes a fund still runs over and they cover it from savings.
- **Not asked:** figures, phone, app locks.

### In their words

- **On funds running over:**
  > "Tuy nhiên, cũng có nhiều lúc bị tiêu lố và phải lấy khoản tiết kiệm ra để bù vào." **(02:22)**

## Interview 3 — second-year student

### Facts

- **Role:** second-year student. Lives in the city, so pays no rent.
- **Money:** about 5,000,000 ₫ a month from family.
- **Spends on:** mainly textbooks, clothes and necessities.
- **Records spending:** no, and has no habit of it.
- **Limit:** none per kind of spending. The 5,000,000 ₫ itself is the limit, and usually not all
  of it is spent.
- **Ran out of money earlier than planned:** yes, in months with electronics, textbooks or
  clothes. Then they cut back on everything else to rebalance.
- **On apps:** expense apps are common, but they would only use one that gives them something
  concrete, not one that is purely for recording.
- **Not asked:** payment method, phone, app locks.

### In their words

- **On app:**
  > "Để em sử dụng thì ứng dụng cần mang lại cho em một quyền lợi cụ thể nào đó (như ưu đãi, thưởng...), thay vì chỉ dùng thuần túy để quản lý chi tiêu, vì bản thân em vốn không có thói quen ghi chép hay để ý các khoản chi." **(01:57)**
  >
  > **English translation:** "For me to use an app, it would need to provide some concrete benefit, such as discounts or rewards, rather than simply being used to manage expenses, because I do not normally have a habit of recording or paying attention to my expenses."

## Method notes for the next round

- Two questions were hypothetical: "would you use an app that…". Ask instead about the last time
  they tried to keep track, and what happened.
- The limit question carried a monthly example, which pushes answers towards months. Give a week
  and a month, or no example at all.
- No Five Whys chain was recorded, and nobody was asked how long an entry takes or when they want a
  warning. For now the survey covers those two.
- Payment method, phone and app locks were not asked, so BR4, BR5, BR6 and BR10 have no interview
  evidence.
